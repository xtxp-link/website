<Aside type="note" title="Deliberately deferred">
The disco's design, plan and implementation come after the MVP. It raises questions of ownership and narrative control: who lists what, who decides what's official, who holds a name. Those deserve their own discussion rather than steering the core protocol. These notes record the shape so far.
</Aside>

## Discovery, not hosting

Hosting a session costs server resources, and XTXP doesn't aim to host everyone's. The ideal is federated and self-organising: people and organisations run their own muppets, and nobody has to ask a central service for permission.

So `xtxp.link/{host}/{path}@{ref}` isn't served by xtxp.link. xtxp.link is a **muppet disco**: it helps a client find the actual muppets hosting the sessions behind an address, and hands it on to them.

- **Anyone can run a disco.** xtxp.link is one among many. Others can run their own, and XTXP couldn't stop them and wouldn't want to.
- **A disco never hosts sessions.** It lists them and points to the muppets that host them.
- **A disco is a starting point for creating sessions.** It can open a new session on an instance of your choosing, including a muppet on your own machine (`localhost`) tied to your key. The new session lives there, not at the disco.

## Queries are signed

A client asks a disco for sessions with a query signed by its key. The signature tells the disco what that key may see, so it can answer with only the sessions visible to that key, under their [grants](/spec/identity/#access-is-granted-by-key).

That keeps lightweight sessions private by default. A [personal session](/spec/sessions/#one-mechanism-for-undo-proposals-and-previews), such as one user's scratch state, need never be advertised at all and may never leave that user's own muppet.

## Domain owners and the bouncer

For an ordinary website, the `@origin` session is the site as its owner publishes it. A domain owner can **claim** it by public key, with a DNS TXT record asserting ownership. Once claimed, the owner decides, at the disco:

- which forked sessions of their pages are listed
- which kinds of edits are permitted where
- what they officially relay

This control is called the **bouncer**. Everyone stays free to fork and host sessions of any page, as the web already lets anyone quote or link to it. The domain owner still controls the official `@origin`, and neither the protocol nor the disco becomes the police.

## Names are contestable

With many hosts and many discos, a session name such as `@alt`, `@punk` or `@notes` on a popular page becomes something people can contest. That needs either a **registrar** for names, or a way for discos to **advertise federation** with each other so a name resolves the same way across them.

## Federation

The rules for how the xtxp.link disco federates are still to be defined. Mastodon's choices on how to use ActivityPub are the closest model: independent instances, each with its own policies, that choose whom to federate with.

ActivityPub itself might even carry disco federation. That's a side quest, deliberately not defined here.

## Still open

- Federation rules for the xtxp.link disco, and whether ActivityPub carries them.
- A registrar for session names, or a federation mechanism that makes names resolve consistently.
- The TXT record format for claiming a domain's `@origin` by key.
- The signed query format, and what a disco learns about a user from it.
- How muppets advertise sessions to a disco, and how listings expire.

**Prior art:** [Mastodon and ActivityPub](/discussion/prior-art/#mastodon-and-activitypub) for federation, and [domain verification by DNS record](/discussion/prior-art/#claiming-a-domain-by-dns-record) for claiming `@origin`.
